Close All Now for Mac

Support

Close All Now sits in your menu bar and quits your open apps in one click. This page covers set-up and the questions that come up most.

Getting started

  1. Open Close All Now. It has no Dock icon. Look for the stacked-squares icon in the menu bar.
  2. Click the icon to see the apps you have open.
  3. Complete the one-time set-up described below.
  4. Click Close Everything to quit all open apps, or the x beside one app to quit just that one.

One-time set-up: the helper

macOS does not let Mac App Store apps quit other apps directly. Close All Now uses a small helper script instead, and macOS only runs it once you have installed it yourself.

  1. Click Install Helper… in the app.
  2. A folder window opens on the right folder. Click Install Helper to confirm.

The script is saved to ~/Library/Application Scripts/com.schwedhelm.closeallnow/QuitApps.js. All it does is pass your quit requests to the apps you chose to close.

Common questions

Will I lose unsaved work?
No. Each app is asked to quit in the normal way, so an app with unsaved changes asks you to save first. Nothing is force-quit.

How do I keep an app open?
Click the shield button beside it. Excluded apps are skipped by Close Everything. You can manage the list in Settings (the gear button). Finder is always left open.

An app did not close.
It is probably waiting for you to answer a save prompt. Switch to that app and respond to it.

How do I start it when I log in?
Open Settings and switch on Start on Login. It is off by default.

How do I quit Close All Now?
Open Settings and click Quit App.

How do I remove the helper?
Open Settings, go to Quit Helper, click Show in Finder and move the script to the Bin.
